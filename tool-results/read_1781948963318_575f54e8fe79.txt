    24→## 1. Project Goals & Success Criteria
    25→
    26→### 1.1 Business Problem
    27→
    28→Sales leadership at a mid-sized Indian pharmaceutical company currently lacks a single, real-time view of how its field force is performing. Data lives in three disconnected systems: the CRM (which records doctor visits and call details), the ERP (which records invoices to distributors and stockists), and an expense system (which records travel & entertainment claims). Quarterly reviews take two weeks of manual Excel wrangling, and by the time the deck is ready, the data is stale.
    29→
    30→The brief asks the agent to simulate this entire ecosystem with a single fabricated dataset, then build a self-serve dashboard that any sales leader can open in a browser to answer five recurring questions: *How are we tracking against target? Are my reps covering the right doctors? Are we over-indexed on one product? Where will next quarter land? Which HCPs are slipping through the cracks?*
    31→
    32→### 1.2 Target Users
    33→
    34→The dashboard has three primary user personas. The **National Sales Head** opens the Overview page first thing every Monday morning to check last week's attainment and YoY trajectory. The **Regional Manager** drills into their zone filter to see which reps and HCPs are underperforming. The **Sales Operations analyst** uses the Field Force and HCP Targeting pages to plan next quarter's call plan and rep territory reassignment.
    35→
    36→### 1.3 Success Criteria
    37→
    38→1. The fabricated dataset covers 36 months (April 2023 → March 2026), contains ≥ 100K total rows across 7 tables, and is fully reproducible from a single Python script with `random_state = 42`.
    39→2. The dashboard runs locally with one command, loads any page in under 2 seconds on a 2020 MacBook Air, and renders cleanly on both 1440p desktop and 1280×800 laptop.
    40→3. All 5 analysis modules are accessible from the sidebar, and every module page surfaces at least 2 of the 8 mandated chart types.
    41→4. All 8 chart types appear at least once across the dashboard, with working hover tooltips and responsive resize.
    42→5. The README explains dataset generation, seeding, dev startup, and every non-obvious decision the agent made.

   113→## 3. Fab Dataset — Overview & Conventions
   114→
   115→### 3.1 Scope
   116→
   117→The dataset simulates **36 months** of operations for MedLife Pharma, a fictional mid-sized Indian pharmaceutical company, covering fiscal years **FY24, FY25, FY26** (Indian fiscal year = April → March). The company has roughly 50 field reps organized into 4 zones, calls on about 3,000 doctors (HCPs) across 12 major Indian cities, and sells 12 products across 4 therapy areas. Total row count across all 7 tables should land between **100K and 120K rows**.
   118→
   119→### 3.2 Naming & Format Conventions
   120→
   121→| Convention | Rule |
   122→|---|---|
   123→| Column names | snake_case, singular (`rep_id`, not `repId` or `reps_id`) |
   124→| Date format | ISO 8601 string `YYYY-MM-DD` in CSVs, `DateTime` in Prisma |
   125→| Currency | INR (Indian Rupee), stored as paise integers (1 INR = 100 paise) to avoid float drift — display layer divides by 100 |
   126→| IDs | String-prefixed codes: `REP-001`, `HCP-0001`, `PRD-CARD-01`, `VIS-20240105-001`, `INV-20240105-001`, `EXP-20240105-001`, `TGT-2024-Q1-REP001-PRDCARD01` |
   127→| NULL handling | Empty string in CSVs (Prisma maps to `null`) |
   128→| Booleans | `true`/`false` literal in CSVs |
   129→| RNG seed | `random_state = 42` everywhere (numpy, faker, pandas) |
   130→| File names | `reps.csv`, `hcps.csv`, `products.csv`, `visits.csv`, `sales.csv`, `expenses.csv`, `targets.csv` |
   131→
   132→### 3.3 Locale Specifics
   133→
   134→All names must come from Faker's `en_IN` locale so the dashboard reads naturally to an Indian user: first names like *Aarav*, *Priyanka*, *Vignesh*; last names like *Sharma*, *Iyer*, *Reddy*; cities limited to **Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru, Chennai, Kolkata, Hyderabad, Pune, Ahmedabad, Jaipur, Lucknow, Kochi, Bhopal**. Phone numbers in `+91-XXXXXXXXXX` format. States restricted to the 28 Indian states + 8 UTs.

   252→## 5. Fab Dataset — Relationships (ERD)
   253→
   254→The dataset is a classic star/snowflake schema with `reps` as the central hub. Below is the textual ERD; implement exactly these relations in Prisma.
   255→
   256→| From | To | Cardinality | Via |
   257→|---|---|---|---|
   258→| `reps` | `reps` (manager) | 1 : N | `reps.manager_id` self-reference |
   259→| `reps` | `visits` | 1 : N | `visits.rep_id` |
   260→| `reps` | `sales` | 1 : N | `sales.rep_id` |
   261→| `reps` | `expenses` | 1 : N | `expenses.rep_id` |
   262→| `reps` | `targets` | 1 : N | `targets.rep_id` |
   263→| `hcps` | `visits` | 1 : N | `visits.hcp_id` |
   264→| `hcps` | `sales` | 1 : N | `sales.hcp_id` |
   265→| `products` | `visits` | 1 : N (via junction string) | `visits.products_detailed` (denormalized CSV — parse in app layer) |
   266→| `products` | `sales` | 1 : N | `sales.product_id` |
   267→| `products` | `targets` | 1 : N | `targets.product_id` |
   268→
   269→The `products_detailed` field is intentionally a CSV string inside `visits` rather than a proper junction table — this keeps the schema lean for a demo project and matches how most pharma CRMs export the field. The app layer must split on `,` and join to `products` as needed.

   273→## 6. Fab Dataset — Generation Rules & Distributions
   274→
   275→### 6.1 Reps
   276→
   277→Generate 50 reps with the hierarchy: 1 National Head → 4 Regional Managers (one per zone) → 8 Area Managers (2 per zone) → ~37 Field Reps (4-5 per area). `hire_date` is uniformly distributed across 2020-01-01 → 2025-12-31, biased toward the middle. Apply **12% annual attrition** — each rep has a 12% chance per year of being marked `Exited` with an `exit_date` somewhere mid-year. Exited reps get replaced by a new rep with `hire_date` = old rep's `exit_date + ~14 days` to maintain coverage.
   278→
   279→### 6.2 HCPs
   280→
   281→3,000 HCPs, distributed across cities proportional to population (Mumbai ~15%, Delhi ~14%, Bengaluru ~11%, etc.). Tier distribution: **A = 20%, B = 50%, C = 30%**. Specialty correlated with therapy area focus: 30% Cardiologists, 30% Diabetologists, 20% Gastroenterologists, 20% Pulmonists. Decile recomputed each quarter based on cumulative revenue.
   282→
   283→### 6.3 Visits
   284→
   285→Each active Field Rep executes **8-12 visits per working day** (Mon-Sat). Working days = total days minus Sundays minus Indian national holidays minus state-specific holidays. Visit-to-HCP assignment is biased: Tier-A HCPs get visited ~3× per month, Tier-B ~1.5×/month, Tier-C ~0.5×/month. Visit type mix: 70% F2F, 15% Virtual, 10% Group_Detailing, 5% Conference. Outcome distribution: 60% Detailed, 25% Briefed, 10% No_Show, 5% Refused. Sample drop counts scale with HCP tier (A gets more samples).
   286→
   287→### 6.4 Sales
   288→
   289→Each Tier-A HCP generates ~2 invoices per month, Tier-B ~1/month, Tier-C ~0.3/month. Invoice value follows a **lognormal distribution** with mean driven by product MRP and HCP tier. Monthly seasonality: peaks in **March (fiscal close)** and **September (festive + half-year close)** at 1.3× baseline; dips in **May (summer slowdown)** at 0.85×. Apply an **8% YoY growth** baseline. New products follow a **6-month linear ramp** from launch date to steady-state. Discounts uniformly 2-12%.
   290→
   291→### 6.5 Expenses
   292→
   293→Each rep incurs ~5-8 expense entries per week. Travel 40%, Food 25%, Lodging 15%, Samples 10%, Other 10%. Amounts: Travel ₹200-₹2,000, Food ₹100-₹800, Lodging ₹1,500-₹4,000. Policy compliance ~95%; non-compliant flagged.
   294→
   295→### 6.6 Targets
   296→
   297→Targets are computed **bottom-up from last year's actuals × growth × stretch**: `target_qty = last_year_actual_qty × 1.15 × rep.target_stretch_pct`. For new products, base on therapeutic-area average. Targets stored at quarter granularity per rep per product.
   298→
   299→### 6.7 Reproducibility
   300→
   301→Set `numpy.random.seed(42)`, `faker.seed_instance(42)`, `random.seed(42)` at the top of the generation script. The same seed must always produce byte-identical CSVs.

   362→## 8. Analysis Module 1 — Sales Performance
   363→
   364→### 8.1 Metrics to Compute
   365→
   366→| Metric | Formula | Granularity |
   367→|---|---|---|
   368→| Total Sales (₹ Cr) | `SUM(net_value_inr) / 1e7` | Daily / Monthly / Quarterly |
   369→| Attainment % | `SUM(actual) / SUM(target) × 100` | Per rep, per zone, per product |
   370→| YoY Growth % | `(current_period - prior_year_period) / prior_year_period × 100` | Monthly |
   371→| MoM Growth % | `(current_month - prior_month) / prior_month × 100` | Monthly |
   372→| Sales by Zone | `SUM(net_value_inr) GROUP BY zone` | Monthly |
   373→| Sales by Therapy Area | `SUM(net_value_inr) GROUP BY products.therapy_area` | Monthly |
   374→| Top 10 Reps by Attainment | `ORDER BY attainment_pct DESC LIMIT 10` | Quarterly |
   375→| Bottom 10 Reps by Attainment | `ORDER BY attainment_pct ASC LIMIT 10` | Quarterly |
   376→| Top 10 HCPs by Revenue | `SUM(net_value_inr) GROUP BY hcp_id ORDER BY DESC LIMIT 10` | Quarterly |
   377→| District/State Heat | `SUM(net_value_inr) GROUP BY state` | Monthly |
   378→
   379→### 8.2 Business Logic Notes
   380→
   381→Attainment % must be computed against **same-period targets**, not annual targets. A rep who hit ₹40L of a ₹50L Q1 target shows 80% attainment, regardless of full-year target. YoY growth for new products (launched within last 12 months) should display as `N/A` rather than `infinity` — handle this in the API layer.

   386→
   387→### 9.1 Metrics to Compute
   388→
   389→| Metric | Formula |
   390→|---|---|
   391→| Total Visits | `COUNT(*) FROM visits WHERE date BETWEEN @start AND @end` |
   392→| Avg Visits / Rep / Day | `total_visits / (active_reps × working_days)` |
   393→| Coverage % | `DISTINCT hcp_id visited / total HCPs targeted × 100` |
   394→| Reach & Frequency | `AVG(visits_per_hcp_per_month)` |
   395→| MCE Compliance % | `mandatory_calls_executed / mandatory_calls_planned × 100` (mandatory = Tier-A HCPs, min 2 calls/month) |
   396→| Samples Distributed | `SUM(samples_dropped)` |
   397→| Visit Type Mix | `COUNT(*) GROUP BY visit_type` |
   398→| Outcome Distribution | `COUNT(*) GROUP BY outcome` |
   399→
   400→### 9.2 Calendar Heatmap
   401→
   402→Aggregate visits by date for the last 12 months. Render as a GitHub-style calendar heatmap (week on Y, day-of-week on X, color = visit count). This is the signature chart on the Field Force page.

   406→## 10. Analysis Module 3 — HCP Targeting & Segmentation
   407→
   408→### 10.1 Segmentation Logic
   409→
   410→- **Tier** (A/B/C) is set at generation time based on revenue potential.
   411→- **Decile** (1-10) is recomputed quarterly: rank all HCPs by 90-day trailing revenue, bucket into deciles.
   412→- **Segment label** is derived from tier × recency: `Champion` (Tier A, visited ≤30 days), `Loyal` (Tier A, visited 30-90 days), `At Risk` (Tier A, visited >90 days), `Emerging` (Tier B, growing), `Slipping` (Tier B, declining), `Long-tail` (Tier C).
   413→
   414→### 10.2 Metrics
   415→
   416→| Metric | Formula |
   417→|---|---|
   418→| Tier-wise Coverage % | `DISTINCT hcp_id visited in tier / total in tier × 100` |
   419→| Decile-wise Revenue Contribution | `SUM(revenue) GROUP BY decile / total × 100` |
   420→| Untapped High-Value HCPs | Tier-A HCPs with 0 visits in last 90 days |
   421→| ROI per HCP | `SUM(revenue from HCP) / COUNT(visits to HCP)` |
   422→| HCP Churn (QoQ) | HCPs whose revenue dropped >50% QoQ |

   427→
   428→### 11.1 Metrics
   429→
   430→| Metric | Formula |
   431→|---|---|
   432→| Product Contribution % | `product_revenue / total_revenue × 100` |
   433→| SKU Mix within Therapy Area | `SUM(revenue) GROUP BY SKU within therapy_area` |
   434→| New Product Adoption Curve | Monthly revenue of `is_new_launch=true` products since launch_date |
   435→| Top 3 Growing Products | `MAX(MoM growth %) LIMIT 3` |
   436→| Bottom 3 Declining Products | `MIN(MoM growth %) LIMIT 3` |

   440→
   441→Treemap showing product revenue contribution nested by therapy area → product → SKU. Pie chart for therapy-area split. Adoption curve as a small-multiples line chart.

   445→## 12. Analysis Module 5 — Forecasting
   446→
   447→### 12.1 Approach
   448→
   449→Use **statsmodels Holt-Winters Exponential Smoothing** (trend + seasonality, 12-month seasonality) on monthly revenue per region and per therapy area. Backtest on the last 3 months: train on months 1-33, predict months 34-36, compute **MAPE**. Then retrain on all 36 months and forecast the next 3 months with 80% / 95% confidence intervals.
   450→
   451→### 12.2 Output
   452→
   453→| Field | Description |
   454→|---|---|
   455→| `forecast_month` | Next 3 months |
   456→| `forecast_value_inr` | Point forecast |
   457→| `ci_80_lower`, `ci_80_upper` | 80% confidence interval |
   458→| `ci_95_lower`, `ci_95_upper` | 95% confidence interval |
   459→| `mape_pct` | Mean Absolute Percentage Error on backtest |
   460→
   461→### 12.3 Visualization
   462→
   463→Single line chart per region with three segments: actual (solid), backtest prediction (dashed), forecast (dotted with shaded CI band).

   937→## 21. Open Questions & Final Notes to the Agent
   938→
   939→### 21.1 Assumptions You Are Allowed to Make
   940→
   941→- Currency is INR. Display in **₹X.XX Cr** for amounts ≥ ₹1 Cr, **₹X.XX L** for amounts ≥ ₹1 L, else **₹X,XXX**. Use Indian digit grouping (1,00,000 not 100,000).
   942→- Fiscal year is **April → March**. Quarters are: Q1 = Apr-Jun, Q2 = Jul-Sep, Q3 = Oct-Dec, Q4 = Jan-Mar.
   943→- The dashboard does not need authentication — assume a single logged-in user with full visibility.
   944→- No PII masking is required (the data is fabricated).
   945→- The "India geo map" should use a TopoJSON file for Indian states. Source it from `india-states.topo.json` in `/public`. A reasonable public source is the [react-simple-maps India example repo](https://github.com/zcreativelabs/react-simple-maps) — verify the license before committing.
   946→
   947→### 21.2 Open Questions (pick a sensible default + document in README)
   948→
   949→1. **Mobile behavior for the calendar heatmap** — collapse to a 6-month view? Pick a sensible default.
   950→2. **Forecast model** — statsmodels Holt-Winters is the default. If you find a simpler moving-average gives lower MAPE on backtest, you may switch and document why.
   951→3. **Date range picker default** — last 12 months is the suggested default. Confirm or pick a different sensible default.
   952→4. **Prisma DB engine** — SQLite is fine for local dev. If you choose to add Postgres support, document the env var swap.
   953→5. **Color palette** — the suggested palette is sky blue (#0EA5E9) for primary, slate (#0F172A) for text, with semantic green/amber/red. You may swap to a different 3-color palette if it improves contrast.
   954→
   955→### 21.3 Final Notes
   956→
   957→- Treat reproducibility as a hard requirement. The same `python scripts/generate_dataset.py` run with seed 42 must produce byte-identical CSVs every time. If you find non-determinism (e.g., dict ordering, set iteration), pin it.
   958→- The dataset is the foundation. Spend time getting it right — every chart depends on it. A typical mistake is to generate `visits` and `sales` independently and lose the causal link (a visit should precede the sale within 14 days). The `gen_sales` function must read from the `visits` table, not generate from scratch.
   959→- The dashboard is a demo, not a production system. Optimize for clarity and visual polish over engineering rigor. If a 200-line Prisma query and a 50-line Prisma query produce the same chart, prefer the 50-line one.
   960→- When in doubt, ship the simplest version that meets the acceptance criteria. You can always iterate.
